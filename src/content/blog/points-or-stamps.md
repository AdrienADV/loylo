A good loyalty program is one your customers can explain to a friend. Before you think about percentages, think about the sentence on your counter: “Your 10th coffee is on us.”

## Start from the visit, not the price

For shops where most tickets look alike, such as a coffee, a pastry or a sandwich, count visits: one point per visit, a reward at ten. For shops where baskets vary, such as a bakery on Sunday or a wine shop, count money: one point per euro, a reward at a round number.

## Make the first reward feel close

Welcome points give a new member a head start. A card that starts at 2 out of 10 gets finished more often than one that starts at zero. In Loylo, you set them once for the program and every new card starts with them.

## Rewards that cost you little

- **A free drink or pastry**: low cost, high perceived value.
- **An upgrade**: a large for the price of a regular, an extra topping.
- **Early access**: the new menu or the seasonal box before everyone else.

## Talk to your members, rarely

A message on the card is a privilege. Keep it for things your regulars want to know: a new product, a change of hours, a double-points week. Loylo allows one message a day per program, so customers never feel spammed.
